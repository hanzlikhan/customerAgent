# DukaanAI — 2-Minute Walk-in Demo Script

*Rehearse this until it feels like a conversation, not a presentation. Total time: ~2 minutes. You need: your laptop with `streamlit run app.py` open on the Customer chat tab, and your phone as backup.*

---

## (a) The 30-second hook (while opening the laptop)

> "Assalam-o-alaikum! I'm Hanzla. Quick question — how many WhatsApp messages do you get a day asking *'yeh kitne ka hai?'* … and how many do you answer late, or miss completely?
>
> I built something for exactly that. It's like a receptionist for your WhatsApp — answers customers in 10 seconds, day and night, from YOUR price list. Let me show you — 2 minutes."

*(Open the laptop. The chat tab is already open on "Chenab Cloth House" — a demo cloth shop like yours.)*

---

## (b) Click-by-click

**Chip 1 — the Urdu price question.** Click: *"lawn ka 3 piece suit kitne ka hai?"*

> "See — a customer typed in Roman Urdu, the way people actually type. Instant answer, with the price. It pulled that from the shop's own catalog — not from the internet, not from its memory."

**Chip 2 — the timing question.** Click: *"What are your timings?"*

> "Same thing in English — it answers in whatever language the customer uses."

**Chip 3 — THE MOMENT. The abstention.** Click: *"do you sell shoes?"*

*(The amber box appears: "I don't have verified information on this, so I won't guess — the shop owner will confirm shortly." + "⏳ Owner notified.")*

> **"This is the most important thing you'll see today.** Every other chatbot would have *invented* an answer here — made up a price, said 'yes we have shoes.' Mine refuses. It says 'I don't know' and tells YOU instead — look, it just landed in the owner's inbox. **It will never quote a wrong price to your customer. Ever.** That's the whole product."

*(Pause. Let that land. This is the close.)*

---

## (c) The dashboard tab (30 seconds)

*(Switch to the 📊 Owner dashboard tab.)*

> "And this is what you see every morning: how many customers it talked to overnight, what they asked, and these —" *(point at the escalation inbox)* "— the 2–3 questions it couldn't answer, waiting for you. Nothing slips through. You answer those yourself, and it learns your catalog better over time."

*(If they seem technical, point at the eval panel:)* "And these numbers are measured, not marketing — 95% of its answers provably come from your catalog, and it has never once agreed with a wrong price in testing."

---

## (d) The ask

> "Here's what I'm proposing: **I set this up for YOUR shop this week.** All I need is your price list — an Excel sheet, or honestly even just a photo of your printed rate list, I'll digitize it. You pay a small monthly fee — less than a quarter of what an assistant costs — and your WhatsApp never sleeps again.
>
> Can I take your price list today and show you YOUR shop running on it by [day after tomorrow]?"

*(If yes: take the photo/spreadsheet on the spot. If hesitant: leave the one-pager — `docs/one-pager.md` — and say:)*

> "No pressure. Here's a one-page summary — my number's on it. When you're answering the same 'kitne ka hai?' for the fiftieth time tonight, think of me."

---

## (e) Objection handling (one line each — calm, never defensive)

**"How much does it cost?"**
> "Less than PKR 10,000 a month — roughly a quarter of one assistant's salary, and it works 24 hours without leave. Plus a small one-time setup while I digitize your catalog."

**"My customers type really weird Urdu — spellings all over the place."**
> "That's exactly what I built it for. Watch —" *(type something with odd spelling, e.g. "sut kitne ka he?", let it answer)* "— it handles the spelling mess because it was tested on it."

**"What if it gives a wrong price to a customer?"**
> "Then I've failed at the one thing it promises. That's why you just saw it refuse instead of guessing — it would rather say 'I don't know' than invent a price. And every answer is traceable to your catalog, so you can always check what it said and why."

**"We already have staff for this."**
> "And they're great at selling — this isn't replacing them. It's the night shift they can't cover: 11 PM to 9 AM, lunch breaks, busy hours when three customers message at once. Your staff closes the sales; this makes sure no customer ever waits."

---

*End state you want walking out: either a price list in your phone, or a one-pager in their hand and your number in their phone. Both are wins.*
